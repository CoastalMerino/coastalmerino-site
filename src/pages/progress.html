  1. 01 · The idea

    A golf polo with nothing synthetic in it.

    Done

    It started on the course. Every polo on the rack was polyester, and the natural options didn't hold up to a real round. We wrote down what the shirt had to be: 100% merino, a collar that keeps its shape without glue, and a fit made for a full swing.

  2. 02 · The farm

    Superfine wool from New Zealand.

    Sourcing

    Merino sheep grow a fiber finer than a human hair. We're sourcing New Zealand merino finer than 18 micron, the range that feels smooth against bare skin. Before launch we'll name where the wool is grown.

  3. 03 · Fabric swatches

    Testing fabric by hand.

    In progress · Fall 2026

    Swatches from several mills are on our table. We're comparing weight, hand feel, and drape, including a classic piqué against a smoother jersey knit, to find the one that's right for eighteen holes and dinner after.

  4. 04 · Swatch to fabric

    One mill, one knit, one weight.

    Next

    Once we choose the fabric, the mill knits sample yardage for our first polos. We'll publish where the yarn is spun and where the fabric is knit.

  5. 05 · Fabric to sample

    Pattern, cut, and sew.

    Next

    The fabric is cut and sewn into the first sample polos. We check the fit, the collar, and every seam, then adjust the pattern and sew another round until it's right.

  6. 06 · Sample to shirt

    Tested on the course.

    Upcoming

    Sample polos go through real rounds, real washes, and real dinners before anything is approved. Only then does the final polo go into production.
